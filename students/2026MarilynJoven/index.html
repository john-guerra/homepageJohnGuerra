Identifying and Characterizing Student Dropout: A Design Study through Dynamic Hierarchies in Visual Analytics

Marilyn S. Joven Fonseca, Marcela Hernández Hoyos, Juan Pablo Reyes, Nicolas Carvajal Chaves, John A. Guerra-Gomez
Master's Thesis — Systems and Computing Engineering Department, Universidad de los Andes, Bogotá D.C., Colombia
Supplemental Materials (OSF)

Student dropout is a persistent problem in higher education, yet the business-intelligence tools institutions rely on report aggregate rates without revealing which subpopulations drive them. We conducted a practice-led design study with administrators at Universidad de los Andes, consolidating 2.8 million longitudinal records into 30,000 undergraduate trajectories (2015 to 2025). The resulting dashboard pairs a reorderable icicle chart with a parallel sets diagram. Its central contribution is a deviation-from-baseline color encoding: at each hierarchical level it maps the signed gap between a subgroup's observed and expected proportion onto a diverging scale that recomputes whenever the analyst reorders the hierarchy. This turns a static layout into a live interface for locating student subgroups whose dropout runs far above or below what their size would predict, without requiring a trained model. Applied to real institutional data, the encoding revealed subgroups that existing reports had never isolated, including a link between a school-level entrance-exam indicator and later credit completion and concentrated dropout along specific program-transfer paths. In a deployment study with 18 practitioners spanning coordinators, faculty directors, deans, vice deans, the system earned a mean System Usability Scale score of 68.47 (SD 13.49; 95% CI [61.76, 75.18]), near the established SUS average of about 68. Participants independently recovered the tool's design-phase findings and surfaced further at-risk groups from their own areas, indicating that a signed, reorderable deviation encoding helps non-expert decision-makers find at-risk cohorts that aggregate reporting hides.
